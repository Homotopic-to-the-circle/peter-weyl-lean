5 Consequences: Characters as Orthonormal Basis and Dimension Identity

5.1 Characters Form an Orthonormal Basis for Class Functions

Definition 24 Class function
#

Uses

  • Definition 1

Lean declarations

  • classFunctions

A class function on \(G\) is a function \(f \colon G \to k\) that is constant on conjugacy classes: \(f(hgh^{-1}) = f(g)\) for all \(g, h \in G\). The space of class functions is denoted \(\mathrm{Cl}(G) \subseteq k^G\).

Theorem 25 Irreducible characters form an orthonormal basis for class functions
#

Uses

  • Definition 24
  • Definition 13
  • Theorem 15
  • Theorem 23
  • Theorem 15
  • Theorem 23

Lean declarations

  • irreducibleCharacters_orthonormalBasis

The characters \(\{ \chi _\xi \} _{\xi \in \hat G}\) form an orthonormal basis for \(\mathrm{Cl}(G)\) with respect to the inner product \(\langle \cdot , \cdot \rangle _G\). In particular, \(|\hat G| = \dim _k \mathrm{Cl}(G) = \# \{ \text{conjugacy classes of } G\} \).

Proof ▶

Orthonormality follows from Theorem 15 (already in Mathlib). For spanning: if \(f \in \mathrm{Cl}(G)\) is orthogonal to all \(\chi _\xi \), then the projector \(\frac{d_\xi }{|G|}\sum _g f(g)\rho _\xi (g)\) vanishes for all \(\xi \). By the Fourier inversion formula (consequence of Theorem 23), \(f = 0\).

The dimension count \(|\hat G| = \# \{ \text{conjugacy classes}\} \) follows since both sides equal \(\dim _k \mathrm{Cl}(G)\) (basis on one side, explicit basis of delta functions on conjugacy classes on the other).

5.2 Multiplicity Formula

Corollary 26 Multiplicity formula
#

Uses

  • Theorem 25
  • Corollary 16
  • Corollary 16
  • Theorem 15

Lean declarations

  • multiplicityFormula

If \(V \cong \bigoplus _{\xi \in \hat G} V_\xi ^{\oplus m_\xi }\), then

\[ m_\xi \; =\; \langle \chi _V, \chi _\xi \rangle _G \; =\; \frac{1}{|G|}\sum _{g \in G} \chi _V(g)\, \chi _\xi (g^{-1}). \]

In particular, two representations are isomorphic if and only if they have the same character.

Proof ▶

Since \(\chi _V = \sum _\xi m_\xi \chi _\xi \) and the \(\chi _\xi \) are orthonormal (Theorem 15), taking \(\langle \cdot , \chi _\xi \rangle _G\) gives \(m_\xi \). Uniqueness of multiplicities implies isomorphism \(\Leftrightarrow \) equal characters.

5.3 Dimension Identity

Theorem 27 Sum of squares of dimensions
#

Uses

  • Theorem 23
  • Theorem 23

Lean declarations

  • sumSquaresDimensions

For any finite group \(G\),

\[ |G| \; =\; \sum _{\xi \in \hat G} d_\xi ^2 \; =\; \sum _{\xi \in \hat G} (\dim _k V_\xi )^2. \]
Proof ▶

Compare dimensions in the isomorphism \(k[G] \cong \bigoplus _\xi \mathrm{End}_k(V_\xi )\) from Theorem 23: \(\dim k[G] = |G|\) and \(\dim \mathrm{End}_k(V_\xi ) = d_\xi ^2\).

Corollary 28 Irreducibility criterion
#

Uses

  • Corollary 26
  • Theorem 15
  • Corollary 26

Lean declarations

  • irreducibilityCriterion

A representation \(\rho \) is irreducible if and only if \(\langle \chi _\rho , \chi _\rho \rangle _G = 1\).

Proof ▶

If \(\rho \cong \bigoplus _\xi V_\xi ^{\oplus m_\xi }\), then \(\langle \chi _\rho , \chi _\rho \rangle _G = \sum _\xi m_\xi ^2\). This equals \(1\) iff exactly one \(m_\xi = 1\) and the rest vanish, i.e. \(\rho \) is irreducible.
