7.3 Irreducible Characters Span \(\mathrm{Cl}(G)\)

The next theorem is the second main result of finite-group character theory after the Peter–Weyl decomposition. We isolate the spanning argument as a separate lemma to enable independent formalisation.

Lemma 54 The projection \(T_f\) associated to \(f \in \mathrm{Cl}(G)\)

ClassFunction.toEndomorphism def:class-functions, def:representation For \(f \in \mathrm{Cl}(G)\) and \(\rho \) a representation of \(G\) on \(V\), define

\[ T_f^\rho \; :=\; \frac{1}{|G|}\sum _{g \in G} f(g)\, \rho (g) \; \in \; (V \to _{\! \ell [k]} V). \]

Then \(T_f^\rho \) is \(G\)-equivariant: \(\rho (h)\, T_f^\rho = T_f^\rho \, \rho (h)\) for every \(h\).

Proof ▼

def:class-functions Compute \(\rho (h) T_f^\rho \rho (h)^{-1} = |G|^{-1}\sum _g f(g)\rho (hgh^{-1}) = |G|^{-1}\sum _{g'}f(h^{-1}g'h)\rho (g')\) (\(g' := hgh^{-1}\)) \(= |G|^{-1}\sum _{g'}f(g')\rho (g')\) (using \(f\) is a class function) \(= T_f^\rho \).

Lemma 55 Schur scalar of \(T_f\) on a simple

ClassFunction.toEndomorphism_simple_smul_id lem:Tf-projection, cor:schur-scalar, def:character, def:Ghat For \(\xi \in \hat G\), \(T_f^{V_\xi } = \mu _\xi (f)\, \mathrm{id}_{V_\xi }\) where

\[ \mu _\xi (f) \; =\; \frac{1}{d_\xi \cdot |G|}\sum _{g \in G} f(g)\, \chi _{V_\xi }(g). \]

In particular \(T_f^{V_\xi } = 0\) iff \(\sum _g f(g)\chi _{V_\xi }(g) = 0\).

Proof ▼

lem:Tf-projection, cor:schur-scalar By Lemma 54 \(T_f^{V_\xi }\) is in \(\mathrm{End}_G(V_\xi )\), which by Corollary 22 is one-dimensional spanned by \(\mathrm{id}_{V_\xi }\). So \(T_f^{V_\xi } = \mu _\xi (f)\mathrm{id}_{V_\xi }\). Take \(k\)-trace of both sides: \(\mathrm{tr}(T_f^{V_\xi }) = |G|^{-1}\sum _g f(g)\chi _{V_\xi }(g)\) on the left and \(\mu _\xi (f)\cdot d_\xi \) on the right; solve for \(\mu _\xi (f)\).

Lemma 56 \(f\) orthogonal to all \(\chi _{V_\xi }\) implies \(T_f^V = 0\) for every \(V\)

ClassFunction.toEndomorphism_eq_zero_of_orthogonal lem:Tf-on-simple, thm:isotypic-decomposition Let \(f \in \mathrm{Cl}(G)\). If \(\sum _g f(g)\chi _{V_\xi }(g) = 0\) for every \(\xi \in \hat G\), then for every \(V : \texttt{FDRep k G}\), \(T_f^V = 0\).

Proof ▼

lem:Tf-on-simple, thm:isotypic-decomposition By Theorem 41, \(V\) is a direct sum of simples \(V_\xi \), and \(T_f^V\) acts diagonally on this decomposition by Lemma 54. By Lemma 55 the hypothesis implies \(T_f^{V_\xi } = 0\) on each summand, hence \(T_f^V = 0\).

Lemma 57 Applying \(T_f\) on the regular representation to \(\delta _1\)

ClassFunction.toEndomorphism_regular_delta_one lem:Tf-projection, def:regular Let \(\rho = \lambda \) be the regular representation on \(V = k[G]\), and let \(\delta _1 \in k[G]\) denote the basis vector at \(1 \in G\). Then \(T_f^\lambda (\delta _1) = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum _{g \in G} f(g)\, \delta _g = \frac{1}{|G|}\, f\), where on the right we identify \(f \in (G \to k)\) with the element \(\sum _g f(g)\delta _g \in k[G]\).

Proof ▼

lem:Tf-projection, def:regular Compute \(T_f^\lambda (\delta _1) = |G|^{-1}\sum _g f(g)\, \lambda (g)\delta _1 = |G|^{-1}\sum _g f(g)\, \delta _g\). Identifying \(\delta _g\) with the basis function \(\mathbf{1}_{\{ g\} } \in (G \to k)\) and recombining gives \(|G|^{-1}f\).

Theorem 58 Irreducible characters span \(\mathrm{Cl}(G)\)

FDRep.span_irreducibleCharacters_eq_top lem:f-ortho-implies-Tf-zero, lem:Tf-regular-delta-one, lem:char-in-cl The irreducible characters \(\{ \chi _{V_\xi }\} _{\xi \in \hat G}\) span \(\mathrm{Cl}(G)\).

Proof ▼

lem:f-ortho-implies-Tf-zero, lem:Tf-regular-delta-one Suppose \(f \in \mathrm{Cl}(G)\) satisfies \(\sum _g f(g)\chi _{V_\xi }(g) = 0\) for every \(\xi \in \hat G\) (this is equivalent to \(\langle f, \chi _{V_\xi ^*}\rangle _G = 0\) for every \(\xi \), where \(V_\xi ^*\) is the dual representation; since \(\xi \mapsto \xi ^*\) permutes \(\hat G\), this is the same as orthogonality to all irreducible characters in \(\langle \cdot ,\cdot \rangle _G\)). By Lemma 56 applied to the regular representation, \(T_f^\lambda = 0\). Apply this to \(\delta _1\): Lemma 57 gives \(0 = T_f^\lambda (\delta _1) = |G|^{-1}\, f\) in \(k[G]\), hence \(f = 0\) in \(k^G\). So the orthogonal complement of the irreducible characters in \(\mathrm{Cl}(G)\) is zero, i.e. they span.

Theorem 59 Irreducible characters are an orthonormal basis of \(\mathrm{Cl}(G)\)

FDRep.irreducibleCharacters_orthonormalBasis thm:char-ortho, thm:char-span, lem:dim-cl-G, def:Ghat \(\{ \chi _{V_\xi }\} _{\xi \in \hat G}\) is an orthonormal basis of \(\mathrm{Cl}(G)\) for the inner product \(\langle \cdot ,\cdot \rangle _G\). In particular,

\[ |\hat G| \; =\; \dim _k \mathrm{Cl}(G) \; =\; |\mathrm{ConjClasses}\ G|. \]
Proof ▼

thm:char-ortho, thm:char-span, lem:dim-cl-G Orthonormality is Theorem 35, hence the \(\chi _{V_\xi }\) are linearly independent. Spanning is Theorem 58. Hence they form a basis. The cardinality identity \(|\hat G| = |\mathrm{ConjClasses}\ G|\) follows by comparing \(\dim _k \mathrm{Cl}(G)\) via this basis with Lemma 52.
