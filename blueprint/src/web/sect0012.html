5.2 Isotypic Components

Definition 38 Isotypic component

Representation.isotypicComponent def:Ghat, def:subrep For \(\rho \) a representation on \(V\) and \(\xi \in \hat G\), the \(\xi \)-isotypic component of \(V\) is

\[ V_{(\xi )} \; :=\; \sum \big\{ \, W \subseteq V : W\ \text{is a $G$-subspace isomorphic to } V_\xi \, \big\} , \]

the sum being taken inside \(V\). It is itself a \(G\)-subrepresentation.

Lemma 39 Isotypic components are pairwise disjoint as subspaces

Representation.isotypicComponent_disjoint def:isotypic-component, thm:schur-dim For \(\xi \ne \xi '\) in \(\hat G\), \(V_{(\xi )} \cap V_{(\xi ')} = 0\) inside \(V\).

Proof ▼

thm:schur-dim, def:isotypic-component, thm:maschke-semisimple Write \(W := V_{(\xi )} \cap V_{(\xi ')}\), a \(G\)-subrepresentation of \(V\). By Maschke (Theorem 19) \(W\) is semisimple, i.e. a direct sum of simples. Each simple summand sits in both \(V_{(\xi )}\) and \(V_{(\xi ')}\), so by the definition of isotypic component is isomorphic to both \(V_\xi \) and \(V_{\xi '}\), contradicting \(\xi \ne \xi '\). Hence there are no simple summands and \(W = 0\).

Lemma 40 Multiplicity space

Representation.multiplicitySpace def:isotypic-component, thm:schur-dim, def:simple For \(V : \texttt{FDRep k G}\) and \(\xi \in \hat G\), define \(M_\xi := (V_\xi \to V)_G\) (the \(k\)-vector space of \(G\)-equivariant maps \(V_\xi \to V\)). Then \(\dim _k M_\xi \) is the multiplicity \(m_\xi \) of \(V_\xi \) in \(V\), and there is a canonical \(G\)-equivariant isomorphism

\[ \mathrm{ev}_\xi : V_\xi \otimes _k M_\xi \xrightarrow {\ \sim \ } V_{(\xi )}, \qquad v \otimes \phi \longmapsto \phi (v). \]

In particular \(V_{(\xi )} \cong V_\xi ^{\oplus m_\xi }\) and \(\dim _k V_{(\xi )} = m_\xi \cdot d_\xi \).

Proof ▼

thm:schur-dim, thm:maschke-semisimple, def:isotypic-component Equivariance of \(\mathrm{ev}_\xi \) is direct. Surjectivity: by Theorem 19, \(V_{(\xi )}\) is a finite direct sum \(\bigoplus _{\alpha } W_\alpha \) of simples isomorphic to \(V_\xi \); pick isomorphisms \(\phi _\alpha : V_\xi \to W_\alpha \subseteq V\), then any \(w \in V_{(\xi )}\) writes as \(\sum _\alpha \phi _\alpha (v_\alpha ) = \mathrm{ev}_\xi \big(\sum _\alpha v_\alpha \otimes \phi _\alpha \big)\). Injectivity: an element of \(V_\xi \otimes M_\xi \) in the kernel decomposes as \(\sum v_\alpha \otimes \phi _\alpha \) for \(\phi _\alpha \) a basis of \(M_\xi \); the requirement \(\sum \phi _\alpha (v_\alpha ) = 0\) together with Theorem 21 (which says the \(\phi _\alpha \) have linearly independent images among simples) forces every \(v_\alpha = 0\). The dimension count follows.

Theorem 41 Isotypic decomposition

Representation.isotypicDecomposition lem:isotypic-disjoint, lem:multiplicity-space, thm:maschke-semisimple For every \(V : \texttt{FDRep k G}\), the inclusions \(V_{(\xi )} \hookrightarrow V\) assemble into a \(G\)-equivariant isomorphism

\[ \bigoplus _{\xi \in \hat G} V_{(\xi )} \xrightarrow {\ \sim \ } V, \]

and consequently

\[ V \; \cong \; \bigoplus _{\xi \in \hat G} V_\xi ^{\oplus m_\xi }, \qquad \dim _k V \; =\; \sum _{\xi \in \hat G} m_\xi \, d_\xi . \]
Proof ▼

lem:isotypic-disjoint, lem:multiplicity-space, thm:maschke-semisimple By Theorem 19 write \(V = \bigoplus _\alpha W_\alpha \) with each \(W_\alpha \) simple. Group the summands by their isomorphism class \(\xi \in \hat G\); the sum of all \(W_\alpha \) in class \(\xi \) is exactly \(V_{(\xi )}\) (it is contained in \(V_{(\xi )}\) and contains every simple summand of \(V\) isomorphic to \(V_\xi \)). The \(V_{(\xi )}\) jointly span \(V\) by construction, and pairwise intersect trivially by Lemma 39; hence they form an internal direct sum decomposition of \(V\). Combine with the description from Lemma 40.
