2.1 The Conjugate of a Linear Map

Definition 11 Conjugate of a linear map

LinearMap.conjugate def:as-module Let \(V, W\) be \(k[G]\)-modules and \(\pi : W \to V\) a \(k\)-linear map. For each \(g \in G\) define the conjugate

\[ (\pi ^g)(v) \; :=\; g^{-1} \cdot \pi (g \cdot v), \]

i.e. π.conjugate g v = (single g⁻¹ 1) \(\bullet \) π((single g 1) \(\bullet \) v).

Lemma 12 Conjugate fixes equivariant inclusions

LinearMap.conjugate_i def:conjugate If \(i : V \to W\) is \(k[G]\)-linear and \(\pi \circ i = \mathrm{id}_V\), then for every \(g \in G\) also \(\pi ^g \circ i = \mathrm{id}_V\) (as \(k\)-linear maps).

Proof ▼

def:conjugate By unfolding: \(\pi ^g(i(v)) = g^{-1}\cdot \pi (g\cdot i(v)) = g^{-1}\cdot \pi (i(g\cdot v)) = g^{-1}\cdot (g\cdot v) = v\), where we used \(k[G]\)-linearity of \(i\) and the retraction hypothesis. This is LinearMap.conjugate_i in Mathlib.
