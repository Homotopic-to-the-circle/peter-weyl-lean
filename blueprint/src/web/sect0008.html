3.3 Matrix-Coefficient Orthogonality (Schur II)

Theorem 27 Matrix-coefficient orthogonality, single irrep

Representation.matrix_coeff_orthogonality_self lem:symmetrise-self-scalar, def:simple Let \(V : \texttt{FDRep k G}\) be simple of dimension \(d := \dim _k V\), and let \(\{ e_1, \ldots , e_d\} \) be a \(k\)-basis of \(V\). Write \(\rho _{ij}(g)\) for the \((i,j)\)-th matrix coefficient of \(V.\rho (g)\) in this basis. Then

\[ \frac{1}{|G|}\sum _{g \in G} \rho _{ij}(g)\, \rho _{kl}(g^{-1}) \; =\; \frac{\delta _{il}\, \delta _{jk}}{d}. \]
Proof ▼

lem:symmetrise-self-scalar Apply Lemma 26 to the rank-one \(k\)-linear map \(A := E_{lj}\) (the matrix unit sending \(e_j\) to \(e_l\), all other basis vectors to \(0\)). Then \(\mathrm{tr}(A) = \delta _{lj}\), so \(c(A) = \delta _{lj}/d\), and reading off the \((i,k)\)-th matrix entry of \(\mathrm{Sym}(A) = c(A)\mathrm{id}_V\) on both sides gives \(\frac{1}{|G|}\sum _g \rho _{ij}(g)\rho _{kl}(g^{-1}) = \delta _{lj}\delta _{ik}/d\).

Theorem 28 Matrix-coefficient orthogonality, distinct irreps

Representation.matrix_coeff_orthogonality_distinct lem:symmetrise-nonzero-implies-iso, def:simple Let \(V, W : \texttt{FDRep k G}\) be simple with \(V \not\cong W\), and choose bases of \(V\) and \(W\). Then for all matrix indices,

\[ \frac{1}{|G|}\sum _{g \in G} \rho _{ij}(g)\, \sigma _{kl}(g^{-1}) \; =\; 0, \]

where \(\rho = V.\rho \) and \(\sigma = W.\rho \).

Proof ▼

lem:symmetrise-nonzero-implies-iso Apply Lemma 25 to any rank-one \(A : V \to W\) and read off the result in coordinates.
